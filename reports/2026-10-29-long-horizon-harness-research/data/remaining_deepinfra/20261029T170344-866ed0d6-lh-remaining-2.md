### New design implications (max 3)

1. **Typed intermediate artifact with deterministic fallback.** From remaining_06 (pp. 5–6): have the LLM emit a schema-constrained record (fields, ranges, enums) that a non-LLM validator normalizes and clips, with a fixed safe default on parse failure. This gives a deterministic verification surface without claiming the LLM's output is semantically correct.
2. **Critic gate as a bounded checkpoint, not a correctness proof.** remaining_06's critic is shown responsive only on hand-crafted adversarial inputs (p. 21, Table 9) and never fires in production; treat such a gate as a risk filter whose own behavior must be tested, and pair it with human checkpoints (remaining_07, p. 7) for irreversible steps.
3. **Re-resolution beats frozen parameters under structural change.** remaining_06 (p. 22) finds the LLM's only statistically separable gain comes from re-resolving abstract tokens against current state at each deploy. For a long-horizon harness, store requirements as re-resolvable references (re-checked at each checkpoint) rather than one-shot frozen instructions — and verify this with an ablation, since the same paper shows no gain under stationary conditions.
