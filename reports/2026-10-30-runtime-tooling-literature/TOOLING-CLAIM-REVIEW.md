## What the implemented system does well

- **Durable intent and evidence:** Session-backed requirements, contracts, results, artifacts and reductions provide an inspectable history and deterministic replay. The manage/preflight/execute/audit/reduce structure distinguishes model proposals from admission and acceptance.
- **Enforced execution boundaries:** Registry checks tier/profile, the active offered bundle, phase/budget, write intent/revision and executing-contract authority. Macro expansion keeps inner Registry checks. Model assertions and an LLM review cannot directly create authoritative closure.
- **Task-sized interfaces:** current audited Lead turns expose 6–10 tools, including bounded result retrieval and controlled macro expansion. This limits prompt/tool-selection burden while retaining useful navigation and staging.
- **Reusable orchestration already exists:** workflows have schema/definition validation, accepted-text digests, tier checks, recorded steps, route pauses and resume memoization. Five registered macros cover common sequences. Roles have separate permission ceilings and routes rather than a mandatory worker swarm.
- **User control already exists:** question cards, checkpoints, staging review, session status, pause/resume, and person-only requirement/artifact decisions are present. A dedicated audited per-predicate UI remains a gap; that does not make all mid-walk interaction absent.

These are useful translations of durable-state, constrained execution, limited-context and provisional-work principles. They are stronger evidence than claiming every element is a direct implementation of a specific paper.

## Tool availability: registration is not permission or reachability

Four distinctions matter: registered tool, offered tool, admitted action, and successful execution. Current audited Lead bundles offer read/navigation, staging, checking and round control; they do not directly offer list_workflows/run_workflow, clerk_submit or delegate. Therefore the existence of a workflow engine or helper profile does not mean an audited Lead can invoke it in its current phase.

`macro_run` is a deliberate offered expansion path with five fixed macros. A write-capable macro needs a person checkpoint, and every inner call still encounters tier, phase, contract and revision checks. Its returned resumable flag does not establish durable exactly-once recovery: the implementation explicitly warns that restarting the macro reruns completed steps. Workflow runs have their own durable done-step memoization, but a crash after an effect and before its done receipt still needs reconciliation/idempotency evidence.

The review should therefore test **reachability and semantics**, not demand a bigger bundle or autonomous swarm. A useful capability matrix includes required tool, phase/profile, authority, nested calls, result handle, retry/cancel behavior and user-visible failure. If workflows should be available during audited execution, add a bounded explicitly designed path with all inner scopes enforced; do not expose arbitrary workflows merely to reduce model tokens.

The audit_projection tool currently constructs its capability manifest from tier/settings, without passing actual resolved routes, selected profile or content roots. Defaults/unknown availability are safer than invented readiness, but do not make it a complete capability view. Derive informational readiness from the same effective context as actual dispatch, while keeping admission authoritative.

## New paper claims that require correction

Citation hygiene also matters in the older-paper comparison. `S0146411625700361.pdf` is *Pattern-Based Approach to the Automation of Deductive Verification of Process-Oriented Programs: Patterns, Lemmas, and Algorithms*; it does not mandate a dynamic taint graph for Librarian. STAGE studies text-attributed graph embeddings rather than a hosted-agent context manager. CoACL's conservative filtering and the knowledge-worker study can inform design by analogy; neither experimentally validates Librarian's promotion rules. Sprint-local labels such as R07 are not stable cross-document paper identities. Retain full title, file hash and page rather than treating the review's labels as citations.

The [paper review](NEW-PAPER-REVIEW.md) gives page anchors and reading order. Key corrections:

- MCP-Secure's 1,080 runs cover two tasks and an internal approver; it does not validate real human approval overhead or supply grain-based revocation semantics. Scoped capability expiry is a design choice to specify and test, not a number to copy.
- WASABI's retry taxonomy and K=1/repeated-failure pattern are well supported. Its issue proportions and Java test thresholds must not become universal production defaults. Its introduction does report previously unknown bugs; a reader's objection to the word “new” was rejected after checking p.2.
- eidos supports coercion/structured rejection and its rendered Figure 2 shows a permission card. Those principles mostly reinforce existing Registry behavior; no wholesale replacement is justified.
- DEMUC's isolated expected object comes from a knowledge model, not a retrieved authoritative source. Its experiments do not prove source entailment, solve context persuasion generally, or validate a universal six-relation batch size.
- Delphi's agreement/false-exclusion figures come from a 100-record sample. APE is a demo with future quantitative evaluation. Neither establishes automatic exclusion/merging safety in this vault.
- InfraMLForge has sufficient methodological ambiguity to exclude it from design authority. Unverified plagiarism claims and ordinary rounding presented as arithmetic errors were rejected.

## Priority recommendations for Librarian's own agents

1. **Make the next admissible action easy to express.** Stable predicate IDs, trusted bound input schemas, deterministic contract skeletons, structured refusal reasons and a requirement/phase progress measure. Repair only semantics-preserving differences and repeat complete preflight. Validate reduced refusal cost without weaker admission.
2. **Bound stalls and explain them.** Track repeated unanswered or answered-but-no-progress clarifications and repeated refusals. Park the dependent requirement, preserve constraints and offer independent authorized work. Report verified predicates, failed/open requirements, blocked reasons and total useful-progress cost; do not reward merely generating more steps.
3. **Qualify the real control path.** Test model-view size over long sessions, tool/bundle reachability, nested workflow/macro checks, cancellation during retries, partial-effect recovery, live resource quotas and durable per-call accounting. Reuse implemented cancellation rather than rebuilding it. Use WASABI-style injected failures with virtual time and configured limits.
4. **Expose existing evidence to the person.** Add a per-predicate inspector with exact checked subject/version, statement, source, current staleness, authority/acceptance distinction and review actions. Broker consequential edits and record their impact. Avoid claiming an unqualified override proves truth.
5. **Evaluate semantic aids separately.** Test a read-only extract/source-isolate/compare reviewer against relationship-inversion, negation, time, units, irrelevant-but-present quotes and insufficient evidence. Keep held-out human labels and negative controls; report benefit, false alarms, cost and latency before HARNESS-08 adoption.
6. **Offer optional screening/review assistance.** Person-approved reversible inclusion rules, MAYBE staging, random exclusion audits, and ambiguity-prioritized human labeling with a random control arm. Do not apply model-generated rules as filesystem access policy or introduce automatic dedupe merges.

No new product algorithm, numerical threshold, role, tool framework or task-completion claim is accepted solely because it appears in a paper. These are recommendations for scoped implementation/evaluation, not changes to the unified plan's counts.
