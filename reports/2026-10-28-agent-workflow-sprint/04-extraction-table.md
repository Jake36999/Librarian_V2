# Methods extracted from the literature (deliverable 3, part 2, revision 2)

Date: 2026-10-28 (revised the same evening after a second-pass review; the first-pass table is in `revision-1/`). Papers are cited by their first-pass rank (#n, see `03-reading-list.md`), section heading and PDF page.

**What changed.** The first pass triaged papers on their first and last pages, read 18 of them with a cap of six method cards each, and its table drew on 13 papers: it left even the highly ranked Embodied Tree of Thoughts without a row and omitted reported costs, negative results and limitations. Revision 2 reads **all 50 distinct papers in full**, one paper per call, with a fixed checklist: problem, evidence type, sample and unit, comparators, methods (categories A to E), costs the paper reports, negative results, limitations and reproducibility notes, each with a page and a verbatim quote. Category E, verification, grounding and deterministic checking, is new: the first pass's A to D had nowhere to put work whose subject is checking an agent's output. The reading produced 1,225 anchor-verified items (313 methods, 200 costs, 183 negative results, 165 limitations, 123 reproducibility, 125 comparators).

**How to read the tables.** Each row is an item the primary reader extracted whose quote a script found on the cited PDF page; the item, its section and page are copied from the data, so the citation is exact. The page cited is the page of the mechanism quote; the **Result** text is the reader's summary from anywhere in the paper (see the reader audit), so a result may sit on another page. The last column, **Applies to Librarian (Hypothesis)**, is mine and is not something the paper claims. The tables are a curated selection of 93 rows (`data/lit2_curation.csv`); every extracted item, with its quote, is in `data/lit2_items.csv`, and the per-paper checklist is in `data/lit2_papers.csv`.

**What the checks show and do not show** (details in the reader audit at the end). A script confirms each quote sits on its cited page. That proves the evidence exists, not that the item supports the label, and it says nothing about methods the reader did not select. A support audit of 60 sampled items and a coverage check against an independent checklist address those two gaps, with limits stated there.

**Evidence limits.** Results are the authors' own, from simulations, benchmarks, case studies or surveys. None evaluates delegation by a closed-weight lead to cheaper models on software work, so the literature supplies metrics, schemas and cautions but no validated cost model for our setting. Surveys (#04, #10, #13, #21) report other people's results; their headline savings (for example #04's "up to 89%") are not experiments. Papers #01 and #12 share authors, method and benchmark and are not independent replications. The vault notes on ReAct, Reflexion, Voyager and similar papers were written from memory and are secondary sources; the primary Reflexion and Tree of Thoughts papers were not retrieved in this sprint (a second-pass review cites them, and I could not check those anchors). Applicability levels are the reader's judgement from the paper's content.

- **Call completion and parse** are separate from quote location: a salvaged answer keeps the complete array items before a defect and drops the rest, so **salvage never proves the reading is complete**.
- **Quote location (exact page)** is stricter than the first pass's one-page tolerance. Re-checking with the strict rule: all 134 first-pass cards labelled verified (103 of 108 from the primary reader, 31 of 60 from the second reader) fall on the exact cited page, as do 25 of 25 of the second-pass reviewer's anchors in this sprint's own text extraction.
- **The strict rule is a floor.** It misses quotes that the PDF breaks across lines at a hyphen ("VLM- related"). A follow-up that compares letters and digits only (`scripts/hyphen_recheck.py`, `data/hyphen_recheck.json`, added 2026-10-29) recovers 104 of the 145 misses: 97.7% of the 1,771 anchored items sit on the exact cited page or run onto the next (MiMo-V2.6-Pro 98.1%, GLM-5.3 98.3%, GLM-5.3-Flash 94.4%), and 30 quotes (1.7%) are found nowhere in the paper. Read the strict rates above as a floor and these as a ceiling. The coverage count below is a floor for the same reason.
- **Semantic support** (does the page support the claim?): a judge from a third model family (Qwen3.5-397B) read 60 sampled exact-page items with their page text: 45 supported, 12 partly supported, 3 not supported, 0 cannot tell. I then read 20 of them myself against the page text: 7 supported, 13 partly supported, 0 not supported; I matched the judge's verdict on 17 of 20 and its supported-or-not direction on 17. Judge verdicts are tier R; mine are the reference for those items. All 13 partial cases had a mechanism on the cited page and result numbers or a detail on the next page or elsewhere; the judge called two items unsupported because its page window missed text that is on the page, and my spot-check of 5 further supported items found 2 that state something about other methods in related work rather than the paper's own method.
- **Where the numbers in result texts come from.** 166 method items carry numeric result text (596 numbers). Every one of those numbers was found in the paper's extracted text (0 were not), but for 95 of the primary reader's 117 such items (81%) at least one number is on a different page from the cited quote. The page cited anchors the mechanism, not the result; `scripts/result_number_check.py` makes this check.
- **Coverage against an independent checklist.** The second-pass reviewer chose 25 passages that the first pass had missed. The primary reader's verified items contain 13 of the 25 by a word-containment rule on the same or an adjacent page (`scripts/coverage_vs_reviewer.py`), and the readers together 16. I then read the 9 passages the rule missed against the readers' items myself (`data/coverage_adjudication.csv`) and counted a passage as found when an item states the same fact, even on another page: 22 of 25 in all. Passages still not found: #7 p. 8 (Verifier cost dominates), #28 p. 6 (Structural dependency checking), #42 p. 9 (Presence does not establish faithfulness). This is a small, selective checklist, so it bounds recall for those passages only; it is not a recall estimate for the papers.
- **Reader overlap.** On the 17 papers read twice, 40 of the primary reader's 128 verified methods (31%) were also found by the second reader (same or adjacent page and at least 30% shared quote words). Overlap between two readers is an exploratory sign of coverage, not a measure of it: independence, equal detectability and the definition of an item are not established. Where the two readers disagree on applicability level: the two readers gave the same applicability level for 13 of 17 papers.
- **Judges.** The applicability opinion (`03-reading-list.md`) and the support audit use Qwen3.5-397B, a third model family, so no judge shares a family with a reader. GLM-5.3 was tried first for the applicability opinion and returned its reasoning instead of JSON until the output cap (a failure the first pass also saw twice), so it was not used as a judge; that call's $0.05 is counted in the spend.
- **What the first pass missed, found again here without being told.** 10 of 11 probes I chose from the items the first pass lacked (and the review named) were found by the primary reader: the 68% extra tokens of reflection (#01); code promised without an archive identifier (#01); a keyword-heuristic rationale score (#01); an unfilled code-repository placeholder (#02); role-level token cost of the verifier (#07); a measured parallel speed-up (#07); a fixed-rule baseline matching the compiler (#14); context utility that is not monotonic (#03); self-check that never overturned a verdict (#08); an offline labelling cost per query (#09).

## What this literature does not supply
No paper in the folder measures delegation economics, orchestrator quota, resume cost or review hold-up on software work. The closest are the drift papers (#01, #12; both report negative transfer outside their home domain), the task-tree validator (#02), the verify-before-act and false-refusal measures (#08), the role-level cost accounting for verification (#07) and the fixed-rule baseline lesson (#14). Verification costs are reported in a few robotics, reward-modelling and agent-system papers; none transfers to text API delegation without measurement (experiments E9, E12, E13 in `07-open-questions-experiments.md`).
