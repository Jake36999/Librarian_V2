# Methods extracted from the literature (deliverable 3, part 2)

Date: 2026-10-28. Each row is a method, metric or schema that a paper in `E:\Librarian research` proposes or evaluates, with its source (paper by reading-list rank, section, page). Every row was read from the PDF text by MiMo-V2.6-Pro, and its verbatim supporting quote was found at the cited page by `verify_anchors.py` (every card used here is `verified`; overall 103 of 108 MiMo cards verified, and only 31 of 60 from the second reader, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, which is why its cards are leads and not evidence; all are in `data/method_cards.jsonl`). The column **Applies to Librarian** is a **Hypothesis** written by me from the measured baseline; it is not something the paper claims.

Reading-list ranks: #01 ReflectiChain: Mitigating Semantic-Execution Drift; #02 Thought Management System; #03 Knowledge-Driven Reasoning for Mobile Agentic AI; #04 Transforming Data Annotation with AI Agents (survey); #05 Towards Communication Efficient Multi-Agent Cooperations; #06 Mutual Influence AI; #08 Confidence-Aware Tree-of-Thoughts; #09 Hierarchical process-level generative reward modeling; #10 Agentic AI: the age of reasoning? (review); #11 HDDLGym; #12 ReflectiChain: Grounding Long-Horizon LLM Planning; #17 Efficient Cooperation-Aware Key and Value Management; #18 Industrial Agentic AI.

**Evidence limits.** Results quoted are the authors' own, from simulations, benchmarks or surveys. None evaluates delegation by a closed-weight lead to cheaper models on software work, so the literature supplies metrics and schemas but no validated cost model for our setting. Surveys (#04, #10) report other people's results and carry less weight. The vault notes on ReAct, Reflexion, Voyager and similar papers were written from memory, are secondary sources, and are not extracted here.

## A. Metrics for agent and orchestration performance

| Method | Source | What it does (authors' mechanism and result) | Applies to Librarian (Hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic Fidelity Index (SFI) and Semantic-Execution Drift (SED) | #01 §6 p10, §2 p4 | Composite 0 to 100 score of instruction adherence over a trajectory: weighted constraint similarity, reinterpretation rate, trajectory consistency and a violation penalty; weights calibrated by AHP with 5 annotators on 20 trajectory pairs. ReflectiChain 88.7 against ReAct 84.4 | Score a delegate output or a session segment against the packet's plan §3.3 constraints. Calibrate on the 8 landed drafts and 2 rejected builder outputs we have |
| TCR, EE, RU | #02 §4 p2 | Task completion rate; efficiency as value per cost; resource utilisation as the share of time agents are active | M2 and M10 analogues. RU resembles the measured mean parallelism of 1.66, but should also count time lost to limits |
| Constraint Compliance Rate (CCR) and Rationale Consistency Score (RCS) | #12 §IV.A p3 | CCR = fraction of steps whose state-action pair satisfies every hard constraint by a symbolic check; RCS = mean NLI entailment of each rationale against the initial constraints | CCR is what a packet lint and diff-scope check would produce for each delegate result (forbidden files untouched, tests named, budget respected). It gives M9 a number |
| Rounds-to-Approval r* | #06 §5.2 p9 | Conversational rounds until the critic outputs APPROVE, computed from logs | Count critic rounds per accepted change next to M4. The two Codex chains used 2 and 1 critic-role calls |
| Dangerous-error rate with false-refusal rate | #08 §III p5 | P(execute given the right answer was refuse), reported with accuracy, false-refusal rate and a threshold frontier, because false executions cost more than false refusals | HARNESS-08 should report a false-"done" rate and a false-"not done" rate with a break-even cost ratio, not one accuracy figure |
| Verification-checkpoint bound | #09 App. A.2 p22 | Detection of an adversarial response rises with verified steps K and depth D; with step-failure correlation the effective K shrinks (measured correlation 0.12 in their data) | Stacked verifiers (anchor check, second reader, tests) count as fewer independent checks than their number. Our readers agree at Cohen's kappa 0.45, so correlation is not small; test in E1 |
| Reasoning steps, reasoning tokens and violations | #03 §IV.A p6 | Cost counted as marked reasoning steps and tokens per episode, with a violation count combining constraint breaches | Report requests and tokens per accepted change together with a violation count (reverts, scope breaches) |

## B. Schemas for deploying and running agents on different kinds of task

| Method | Source | What it does | Applies to Librarian (Hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verify-before-act 5-tuple | #08 §III p4 | Output is a JSON verdict (label EXECUTE or REFUSE_*, evidence box required for EXECUTE, pre-state, post-state, reason) | A delegate "accept" or "ship" verdict must carry an evidence anchor, enforced by the verifier (DELEG-02). The encoder critic's SHIP verdict cited functions and tests in prose, not as anchors a script could check |
| Planner, annotator, checker loop with escalation | #04 §2.3 p6 | Decompose, label, audit; failed audits are escalated for re-annotation | In mapper, builder, critic chains, escalate to the orchestrator at the first failed audit instead of re-running the builder (builders were accepted 0 of 2 times) |
| Cascade routing | #04 §10.6 p29 | Cheap models handle easy items and high-compute models only hard ones; the survey cites offloading savings up to 89% from other work | Our lanes already cascade (triage by a cheap model, deep reads by the primary reader). Routing rules by task type are in `05-deployment-patterns.md` |
| Two-round role orchestration, JSON only | #06 §4 p5 | Baseline round, feedback wiring, one influenced round; each agent emits one JSON object; 167 runs, 1,822 requests, $2.05, cost linear in agents and rounds | Cap critic loops at two rounds with typed output, so cost is predictable before dispatch |
| Agent-centric HDDL domain | #11 §4 p3 | Domains declare agent types, agent-parameterised actions and task effects that are checked against world state | Write packet acceptance as declared, checkable effects (tests named, files changed), which is what plan §3.3 asks for but nothing enforces |
| Three-tier hierarchical verification | #09 §4.4 p5 | Global reasoning, then step-level verification, then an integrated judgement; ablation 82.3% (full) against 74.2% (tier 1 only) on RM-Bench | Keep the tiers separate: anchor check, label review, executed test. Each adds something in their ablation |
| LLM-BDP four-agent decomposition | #05 §A p8 | Per robot: feature ranking and bandwidth-adaptive agents for messages, navigation and collision-avoidance agents for actions; the budget enters through the prompt | Put budget fields (tokens, cost, time) in the packet so the delegate can plan within them |
| Four-stage agent framework | #10 §1.2 p2 | Data, analysis (validate input, access memory, verify decisions), execution, adaptation (feedback into memory) | Resembles HARNESS-03's manage, preflight, execute, audit, reduce. A survey framework without experiments, so low weight |
| Edge-cloud hybrid and ensemble aggregation | #18 Case 1 pp5 to 6 | Low-latency inference on edge GPUs, training in the cloud; per-instance model ensembles feed a group model | Loose analogy to local scripts against hosted compute and to an aggregator over independent reviewers. No data; low priority |

## C. Long-horizon planning, drift and memory management

| Method | Source | What it does | Applies to Librarian (Hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drift propagation equation | #01 §3.1 p5 | D(t+1) = alpha D(t) + epsilon(t) + beta P(t); alpha estimated by OLS with robust errors; stable when alpha < 1. ReflectiChain alpha 0.823 (95% CI 0.794 to 0.852) against ReAct 1.047 | Fit alpha to a per-step conformance score (CCR or SFI per delegate output) along a lane; alpha at or above 1 flags a drifting lane. Needs scores we have not collected yet |
| Retrospective reflection with re-anchoring | #01 §4.1 p7 | Compare the current rationale with the initial constraints by embedding similarity; below 0.85, rewrite and re-anchor. Ablation: removing it costs 30.3 RCS points | The resume capsule (DELEG-04) carries the active task's acceptance text, and the diff is re-checked against it before commit. Cold resumes otherwise re-derive state (135k to 502k tokens) |
| Task tree, entity schema and validator | #02 §4.3 p5, §4.2 p5, §4.1 p4 | A predefined tree of goals with dependencies; a validator checks each result against the expected outcome, explains the decision and stores a condensed output; their marketing simulation shows error falling from 17% to 5 to 7% | The plan's task table is the tree. The validator is the closure check (DELEG-05); the condensed output is the receipt summary |
| Three-tier memory | #10 §3.3.2 p11 | Parametric, working (context window) and external memory, coordinated by a controller | Keep working context short (peak 966k tokens; a post-limit rebuild cost 502k) and put durable state in external memory: capsule, receipts, plan |
| Adaptive depth allocation | #09 §6.3 p7 | Spend more verification depth on harder items | Allocate verifier effort by task class: golden-fixture refactors need little, concurrency-sensitive changes more. Consistent with the measured follow-up spread (Claude median 14.9k; Codex p90 246k tokens) |
| Drift monitoring with human override rate | #04 §10.7 p30 | Dashboards of human override frequency and error trends to catch gradual degradation; stated as future work, not evaluated | User corrections per accepted change, from the transcripts, as a drift signal. Not measured yet |

## D. Communication-efficient multi-agent cooperation

| Method | Source | What it does | Applies to Librarian (Hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selective broadcast under a hard budget | #05 §II.B p4, §B p7 | A binary selection vector chooses which human-readable features to send, with message size held under a budget; learned by constrained MAPPO with a Lagrange penalty. Messages shrank from 80 to about 32 bytes (60%) at equal reward in simulation | Delegate packets carry only anchored, task-relevant excerpts under a token budget. Test whether shorter packets change the hold-up rate (new experiment E7) |
| Cacheable knowledge synchronisation | #03 §IV.C p8 | Send a reusable knowledge pack once (125 to 1,234 tokens) instead of a 1,870-token closed-loop exchange per disruption | A stable cacheable prefix (contract, repository map) plus a small variable suffix. Cache reads cost about 4% of fresh input on the client's own price records, so reuse is worth roughly 25 times; `reports/2026-10-26-adapt09-prompt-layout.md` already studies layout |
| Orchestration layer against dialogue only | #02 §5.8 p14 | With a task-management layer: task completion 81.0%, 5 consensus rounds, 22 coordination turns, against 66.5 to 71.2% completion for dialogue-only or search-only coordination | Orchestration calls are 12.6% of Codex tokens; measure coordination turns per accepted change before and after adding an explicit task tree |
| Request, inject, verify | #03 §IV.B p6 | The agent pulls a knowledge pack only when a disruption invalidates its plan; the pack is injected and cached; candidates pass legality checks before commit | Pull-based context for delegates (ask for more files on a verifiable need) rather than pushing 100k-token packets. Unmeasured |
| Sliced Shapley value for contribution | #17 §4.1 p5 | Credits each cooperating unit with its expected complementary contribution to global utility, estimated by Monte Carlo sampling with an error bound. The units are attention heads | Could credit mapper, builder and critic lanes for accepted outcomes if lane ablations existed. We have none; low priority |

## Gaps this literature does not fill
No paper in the folder measures delegation economics, orchestrator quota, resume cost or review hold-up on software work. The nearest are #01 and #12 (drift metrics) and #02 (task-tree validation). Metrics A1 to A7 and schemas B1, B2 and B5 are the usable core; D1 and D2 are the only communication-efficiency results with a direct cost analogue (message size, cache reuse).
