# How agents use and build the Librarian: summary of the research sprint

Date: 2026-10-28. Everything here is measured from receipts, ledgers, git history and the harnesses' own session logs, or marked as reported. Files: `01` metrics, `02` baseline, `03` reading list, `04` extraction table, `05` deployment patterns, `06` design proposals, `07` open questions, `08` claim register, `data/` and `scripts/` (re-runnable), `STATE.md` (resume capsule). Nothing was committed; no product code changed.

## What the evidence says

1. **The binding constraint is orchestrator quota, not dollars.** DeepInfra spend on development was about $0.70 in five days. Claude was locked out for 62 hours (weekly limit hit 2026-10-26T01:00Z, reset 2026-10-28T15:00Z) after two 5-hour limits. Codex hit 59 unique limit turns in nine days, in bursts of a median 28.5 minutes followed by a median 4.6-hour wait. In the 103-hour window both agents were blocked for up to 59 hours (57%).
2. **Resuming is expensive because of the cache, not the reading.** After a limit stall Claude's first request rebuilds its cache: a median 502k fresh tokens (n=3). Codex's main thread pays 135k. The plan a fresh session would read grew from 13k to 31k tokens (§19 alone 17.6k). Cache reads are 99% of Claude's input tokens.
3. **Delegation produced no visible orchestrator saving.** After a delegate result the orchestrator spent a median 14.9k (Claude) and 33.4k (Codex, p90 246k) tokens, about 6 to 8 times the delegate's output. Claude changes with a delegate involved cost a median weighted index of 603k against 701k without (14 against 10, overlapping). The counterfactual cannot be observed.
4. **Delegates did well where acceptance was mechanical and badly where it was not.** Claude landed 9 delegate-drafted commits with 0 to 2 recorded fixes, the adapter ones checked by golden fixtures. Codex's two mapper, builder, critic chains got no accepted builder output (one patch rejected, one call timed out); the mapper and critic roles were useful.
5. **Review findings held up about half the time in pooled commit records (6 of 12, Wilson 25 to 75%), but the split is stark:** Claude-side 6 of 8, Codex encoder review 0 of 4. Findings are not stored as data, so the "four findings" in commit `33fb3b5` cannot be matched to a receipt.
6. **The delegation tooling has measurable defects.** One finished call stayed `started` in the ledger; a retry began before the first completed; 4 of 9 completed calls hit the output cap; 3 of 8 rate cards are stale (GLM-5.3 is twice the card); per-call receipts for 10-24 to 10-27 are missing.
7. **Gemini's review is partly right, partly duplicates existing parts.** Its 943k compaction is real (942,961 tokens dropped in a research session). Its cost argument about re-processed history is overstated (cache reads cost about 4% of fresh input). Most of its proposed components already exist in some form (`tools/delegate.py` fans out read-only helpers; `broker.py` parks unanswered questions; `coordination.py` has leases), and `pace.py` is `set_effort`, not pacing. Quota awareness, an executable gate, a resume capsule and a drafter-not-reviewer check are absent everywhere. Of 58 genuinely outside claims: 3 verified, 22 plausible, 33 unverified (nine more were local claims, checked against the project's own data); the OpenAI Navier-Stokes figures are plausible from secondary reports because the primary announcement page was not retrievable.
8. **Hosted compute prior art (`G:\vast`):** 31 jobs for $13.62, but two of six boxes produced nothing and ran about 14 hours (an estimated third of spend) after an error had been logged.

## What I propose (details and plan rows in `06-design-proposals.md`)
DELEG-01 runner hygiene and receipt v2; DELEG-02 typed, anchored delegate output with a local verifier; DELEG-03 packet lint and diff-scope guard; DELEG-04 resume capsule (at most 4k tokens); DELEG-05 auto-recorded delegation lines and a closure check; then two experimental items, DELEG-06 executable gate and DELEG-07 limit-aware scheduling, each with a kill criterion. One runtime item, HARNESS-10, makes helper results typed and anchored. I do not propose new FanOutDispatcher, LeaseWatchdog, DeferredQuestionQueue, EngagementPlanner or telemetry components.

## What the sprint itself taught about verification
- Mechanical anchor checks work: 1,963 model-extracted items, 96.3% had their verbatim quote at the cited place (MiMo-V2.6-Pro 99.2%, GLM-5.3 98.6%, GLM-5.3-Flash 97.4%, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 91.5%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro **64.5%**). A reader below about 90% is a source of leads, not evidence.
- Anchors prove the evidence exists, not that the label is right: two "present" labels for quota-aware dispatch described static caps, and the reader's own gap note said so.
- Failure classes to design for: 20% of calls truncated at the output cap, 3 of 11 triage batches with one unclosed JSON object per item (repairable), 2 of 6 GLM-5.3 runs that leaked reasoning into the answer. Precision is cheap to automate; recall is not (a single MiMo pass recovered about 71% of events a two-reader estimate says were findable).
- **Self-metrics for this sprint.** DeepInfra: 87 calls (85 extraction, 2 critic), 2.72M input and 0.68M output tokens, $2.45 at live prices ($2.07 by the runner's stale cards), no failures. Orchestrator (this session, Sonnet 5.5; measured to 20:44Z, 117 messages in about 4.5 hours): 1.37M fresh, 0.51M output and 59.4M cache-read tokens, context peaking at 816k; the session ran on while the reports were audited. Scripts did the counting; delegates did the reading; but one long session still accumulated most of the cache-read cost, which is the argument for DELEG-04.
- **Independent critic pass** (two readers, $0.04, 32 findings, every quote machine-checked; adjudication in `data/critic_adjudication.csv`). 13 findings were real defects from 7 underlying problems, 14 were numbers that were right but had no summary file, and 5 were false positives. The defects included a regex in my own git script that undercounted delegate-drafted commits (6 reported, 9 real, which moved the Claude with-delegate comparison from 12 against 12 to 14 against 10) and a verifier that overwrote its own table on partial runs. All five false positives came from support missing from the critic's packet or a cross-session comparison, so give a critic the whole data folder. Both critics found the same top defect independently. Two further defects were found by reconciling against independent counters, which the critics did not catch: Codex logs some token events twice (308 duplicates, about 2.5% of Codex tokens, now removed) and this sprint's own delegate calls had leaked into the Claude follow-up statistic (31 real invocations, not 34).

## Limits of this work
n is small throughout (12 dispositions, 23 Codex bursts, 3 Claude post-limit resumes). Codex idle time includes your absences, so the both-blocked figure is an upper bound. Model-labelled transcript events (802) are tier R: anchors verified, labels not individually adjudicated. Claim tags come from hand-written rules; a spot-check of the verified rows and the broadest rules corrected one tag (the OpenAI manuscript count, verified to plausible), but the 33 default-unverified rows were not re-read one by one. The research-side batch scripts, past per-call Claude delegations and the OpenAI primary page were not available. Past packets were not stored, so packet-contract conformance (M9) is not measurable yet.
