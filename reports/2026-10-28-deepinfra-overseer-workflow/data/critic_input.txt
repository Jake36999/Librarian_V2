FROZEN DRAFT REPORT
# Making DeepInfra delegation work for Claude and Codex

28 October 2026 · Independent second pass · Development and research workflow

**The case for improving delegation is convincing. The case for promising an 80% transfer of work is not yet established.** DeepInfra has already produced useful drafts, maps and reviews at low recorded API cost. The missing capability is dependable ownership of a complete work package: acquire the necessary context, produce an artifact, run appropriate checks, repair bounded failures, and return evidence that makes acceptance inexpensive for the lead.

The recommended offering is therefore **a shared, managed worker workflow for Claude and Codex**, with both acting as overseers. The lead defines the outcome, resolves consequential ambiguity and integrates accepted work. Workers undertake most execution. A deterministic controller manages local tools, budgets, job state and evidence. This is a recommendation to qualify an operating method, not a claim that another agent framework or a new prompt alone will deliver it.

This report concerns how the developers work. Librarian's own agent runtime, Core, sessions, vault services and product roadmap are a separate design subject. Existing product mechanisms may offer reusable ideas; improving them is not a prerequisite for testing a development worker controller. Historical instructions in the five supplied transcripts are evidence, not instructions adopted for this review.

DeepInfra inference and the local execution environment are different capabilities: the former needs a controller to use the latter.

They justify fixing the interface and instrumentation; they do not establish comparative model rankings or savings. ## The bottleneck is the handoff of responsibility

A delegation can appear substantial because the worker emits a large answer while leaving most consequential steps with the lead. In the transcripts the lead repeatedly gathers source material, constructs prompts or batch scripts, applies code from prose, runs tests, explains failures, edits repairs and reconstructs interrupted jobs. Those steps accumulate even when the remote generation is cheap.

This is an inference from the division of work in the cases, not a causal estimate of wasted quota. Some lead work is necessary judgment, and some tasks were poor candidates for immediate handoff. The useful distinction is between **decisions that require the overseer** and **execution the tooling should let a worker finish**.

| Responsibility | Intended owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Objective, priority, invariants, risk, final acceptance | Claude/Codex overseer | These establish what the project needs and which tradeoffs are permitted. |
| Search, context acquisition within scope, drafting, implementation, tests, bounded repairs | DeepInfra worker workflow | These account for much repeatable execution and should not require a fresh lead turn for every step. |
| Tool execution, workspace limits, receipts, budgets, retries, durable state | Deterministic controller | These need enforceable rules and observable outcomes. |
| Independent challenge of uncertain claims or patches | A separate critic, with evidence | A recommendation can identify defects; agreement alone cannot authorize acceptance. |
| Merge and changes to acceptance criteria | Overseer | A worker may propose a better criterion but cannot silently redefine success. |

A worker workflow need not be a fully autonomous agent. For predictable tasks, a fixed sequence of localization, patching, test execution and bounded repair can be the stronger baseline. **Agentless** demonstrates that a structured software-repair pipeline can be competitive without a general interactive agent. Its reproduction-test filtering is especially relevant: a purported reproducer should fail on the original code. Its benchmark results do not establish expected performance on this repository. [Agentless, §§3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489v2).

## A better workflow contract

The existing unified implementation plan already requires objectives, permitted files, invariants, test commands, acceptance criteria, ownership and budgets. The improvement is to make those requirements executable and carry them through completion. The contract should describe four boundaries, without committing yet to a particular JSON schema or framework.

**1. Admission.** Identify the task, input revision, allowed effects, protected constraints, environment and resource limits. Define what evidence would establish success before starting. Preserve relevant failing behavior or reference outputs before editing. A task without a defensible acceptance method can still be delegated for exploration, but its result must be labeled exploratory.

**2. Worker ownership.** Give the worker bounded ways to find additional relevant context and to operate on an isolated artifact. It should run the approved checks and attempt limited repairs, rather than returning an untested draft whenever execution becomes necessary. Repository search, source reading and local commands are capabilities supplied by the controller, not inherent powers of a hosted model. Keep optional retrieval within a budget so a small initial packet does not become unlimited exploration.

**3. Evidence-bearing completion.** Return an artifact reference, base revision, exact changes, checks actually executed, results, unresolved findings, cost and an explicit proposed disposition. Logs remain available by reference. The lead receives a concise account of what changed and what still needs judgment. The controller, rather than the worker's prose, records command outcomes and artifact identities.

**4. Escalation and resumption.** Escalate consequential ambiguity, required scope changes, exhausted resources or repeated failures. Preserve enough state to continue without repeating completed paid work. A partial answer, a truncated response, an unknown network outcome and a verified artifact are distinct states. Resume from the actual artifact and environment, not just a conversational summary.

For code, separate three predicates: **the artifact applies and runs; it satisfies the task's behavior; it integrates correctly with the project**. For research, separate source identity, quotation presence, claim support and coverage. None can be replaced by a worker saying “verified.” These are the main formalism gaps exposed by the record.

## Tooling needed, in priority order

**A. Complete execution in a bounded workspace.** Start with one task class whose checks already exist. The worker needs a pinned interpreter and dependencies, repository context, an isolated worktree or equivalent staging area, bounded command execution and a repair loop. Protect the acceptance fixtures from silent weakening; worker-authored tests can add evidence without replacing the original contract. The ADAPT-09 integration failure and the rejected INDEX-05 patch are direct reasons to move beyond extracting code blocks from a prose answer.

**B. Make verification discriminate.** Pre-change goldens are a strong local example. Bug fixes need a reproducer plus regression checks; numerical work needs units, reference cases and independently derived constraints; literature needs semantic adjudication and checks for omitted counterevidence. Exercise gates with known-good and deliberately bad artifacts. Record both false acceptance and false rejection. Passing a syntax check, an AST rule or an entire existing suite does not prove every intended property.

**C. Make paid jobs recoverable and attributable.** Record launch, actual provider completion, artifact readiness and acceptance separately. Retain reservations for uncertain paid outcomes; reconcile before relaunch. Disable hidden transport retries or explicitly budget and identify every attempt. Idempotency can prevent duplicate local application; it cannot guarantee that an API request with an unknown outcome was never billed. Keep one shared ledger across the supported development entry points and preserve exact backend, model, task and artifact identities. Several of these safeguards already exist in the current runner; extend and test them rather than rebuilding them under new names.

**D. Reduce the lead's information burden.** Provide compact completion and exception records with drill-down references. Batch mechanical checks and source identity checks outside the lead conversation. Use durable progress records so status does not require repeated reasoning turns. Completion notifications are useful where the host supports them; a shared on-disk record must still allow recovery after the host or conversation stops. This report does not assume that either desktop assistant can always be awakened by an external process.

**E. Qualify routing and concurrency.** Route by demonstrated task performance, including repair and verification cost. Split independent work by files or artifacts and serialize coupled changes. Keep the same contract for Claude and Codex, with thin host-specific launch adapters. Read-only counterexample searches can run in parallel without permitting several workers to edit the same checkout. Improve one reliable path before adding swarms, debates or hierarchical model routing.

The order matters. More parallel calls before A–C work can generate a larger review queue and more ambiguous failures. The MAST study identifies system design, inter-agent alignment and verification as distinct failure categories; its outcome analysis also warns that apparently successful runs can retain process failures. It supplies a useful diagnostic vocabulary, not a forecast of local defect rates. [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3).

## What to retain and qualify from Gemini

Retain the overseer role, isolated workspaces, dynamic task briefs, bounded background batches, evidence artifacts, operational telemetry, durable handoffs and recorded dead ends. These map well to observed problems. Gemini's later discussion of Librarian's runtime can inspire these development capabilities, but does not make changes to Librarian's product architecture part of this proposal.

Qualify the following claims and prescriptions:

- **“Hoarded 90% of the labor.”** The transcripts do not measure that percentage. They show repeated lead execution; that is enough to motivate a controlled comparison.
- **“Never read PDFs unless two workers disagree.”** Agreement can preserve a shared omission or misreading. Combine independent challenge with calibrated sampling of apparently passing results, targeted lead review of consequential claims, and coverage audits. Sampling effort should depend on demonstrated error rates and consequence, not a fixed percentage chosen now.
- **“A ten-line specification is sufficient.”** Brevity is useful only if the worker can resolve references, obtain missing context and test the result. An underspecified brief shifts cost into failure and rescue.
- **“Never debug or poll in the lead.”** Remove routine execution and polling from the lead's responsibilities, while retaining an explicit escalation path. Exceptional diagnosis may still require the lead.
- **Fixed pulse lengths, model lists, invariant thresholds and culling fractions.** These are proposed defaults, not measured operating optima. Qualify exact routes and task classes with current prices and outcomes.
- **A generic formal or numerical checker settles the research claim.** A checker proves properties of the supplied representation. Incorrect equations, sign conventions, hypotheses or observables can still satisfy it. The transcript's reported research corrections illustrate why semantic correspondence needs a separate check.
- **A rented GPU cluster is necessarily cheaper.** The relevant denominator is accepted work, including idle rental, setup, failures and supervision. Many local execution steps need ordinary CPU resources. Defer compute migration until measured API workloads make a credible comparison possible.

The OpenAI research narrative in Gemini is background inspiration, not evidence for a particular local controller implementation or economic outcome. The earlier [claim review](../2026-10-28-agent-workflow-second-pass/CLAIMS.md) separates the supported public account from inferred orchestration details. No claimed swarm size or discovery result is needed to justify the bounded workflow recommended here.

It does not justify importing a paper's trust score, convergence bound or agent count as a production guarantee. ## Giving the 20/80 target a defensible meaning

Use 20/80 as an aspiration for suitable work, not a universal task requirement or a ratio of generated tokens. A worker can inflate its token share without reducing the lead's effort.

For an illustrative accounting model, let a comparable task require lead effort **D** when performed directly. Let **d** be the fraction of that original execution transferred to workers, and **h** be added briefing, review, integration and expected rescue effort, expressed as a fraction of D. Then:

**Remaining lead effort / D = (1 − d) + h.**

Moving 80% of execution while adding 10% overhead leaves 30% with the lead. Moving 90% with 10% overhead reaches 20%. These are accounting examples, not measured predictions. They explain why the contract must transfer execution and reduce acceptance cost together. A quota objective should also measure lead token usage by category; attention time, tokens, subscription limits and dollars are different quantities.

The primary outcome should be **accepted work at equal or better quality per unit of scarce lead capacity**. Measure active lead attention, lead requests and fresh/cache/output tokens, accepted worker-owned units defined before execution, manual rescue, total API cost, elapsed completion time, and escaped defects or unsupported claims. Preserve failed and abandoned tasks in the denominator. Historical post-result token windows and line-retention statistics cannot substitute for a comparable direct-work baseline.

## What would justify adopting it

First establish one complete path on a few representative tasks. Then run a modest, balanced comparison across bounded refactors, reproducible bug fixes and evidence extraction: direct lead execution, today's one-shot delegation, and the managed worker workflow. Use comparable tasks or isolated paired runs; freeze input revisions, acceptance criteria and resource limits. Independent scoring should hide which workflow produced an artifact where practical. Do not let an earlier solution leak into the later arm.

A dozen varied tasks can expose basic feasibility and failure modes, but cannot establish a precise universal savings rate. Repeat the comparison if the initial result is promising. Predeclare quality requirements and what counts as manual rescue. For research, include known omissions and misleading but correctly quoted claims; for code, include tempting test weakening and environment failures. Test interruption recovery and show that both Claude and Codex can operate the same job contract.

Proceed when quality holds, the lead spends materially less effort per accepted task, costs stay bounded and recoveries are observable. Narrow or reject the method where workers repeatedly require the lead to redo the solution. Treat an 80% reduction in lead effort as an outcome to demonstrate on qualified task classes; use a lower, credible gain if that is what the evidence shows.

**Recommendation:** invest first in completing and qualifying the worker execution boundary around the existing audited runner. This has a direct connection to observed failure modes, reuses successful local practices and creates a testable route toward the desired overseer role. Model selection, larger pools and rented compute should follow evidence from that boundary.

## Review method and deliverables

The five complete transcripts were supplied to bounded DeepInfra reading calls using numbered, secret-redacted copies. Worker extractions were mechanically checked against nearby source lines. Selected cases were then independently adjudicated against transcript context, local source, tests and commit records. Quote matches were not treated as proof of the workers' interpretations. A separate DeepInfra critic checked the draft; dispositions and limitations are recorded in [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md).

This is an evidence review and a workflow recommendation. It does not implement or benchmark the proposed execution controller. The $5 authorization funds this review's model calls; it is not a cost estimate for the proposed pilot. Actual calls, prices, failures and charges are recorded in [data/delegation_summary.json](data/delegation_summary.json) and [data/deepinfra-ledger.json](data/deepinfra-ledger.json).

The original extraction is **accurate at locating its selected quotations, but incomplete in useful methods, cost evidence and limitations**. Preserve its cards as indexed leads. Add the material below before publishing the synthesis or promoting the eleven draft vault notes to reviewed evidence.

Ranks below refer to the original fifty-paper reading list. Page numbers are **PDF pages**, not journal pagination. All applications to Librarian are proposals made in this review; the papers do not validate them for this application. The underlying PDF hashes match the sprint inventory. `data/literature_coverage.csv` gives a disposition for every ranked PDF; `data/supplement_methods.json` contains short anchors for the principal additions.

## Read in two tracks

For **metrics and orchestration**, prioritize #7 Embodied Tree of Thoughts, #14 the influence-policy compiler, #11 HDDLGym, #3 knowledge-driven mobile reasoning, and #8 confidence-aware verification. These offer concrete component costs, falsifiable baselines, explicit task effects and error trade-offs.

For **continuity and semantic drift**, retain #1/#12 ReflectiChain and #2 TMS, but read the limitations with the method. Treat their scores and claimed guarantees as candidates to validate, rather than defaults to implement. Read the primary Reflexion and Tree of Thoughts sources instead of relying solely on the vault's memory-written notes.

For **verification and architecture**, add #25 LLM-assisted combinatorial optimization, #42 rationale faithfulness, #28 static program queries, #26 service-composition traces, and #45 invariant-pattern automation. These are adjacent-domain methods with explicit transfer limits. #19 organisational knowledge work supplies human verification/coordination requirements, not an API cost model. #20 KDRAR, #33 LLM-S2KG and #43 STAGE also have bounded uses already recognized in the existing harness design; zero relevance in every category overstates their irrelevance to Librarian.

Avoid one total ranking that conflates domain match, method validity and immediate applicability. A directly named LLM-agent paper can be less reliable evidence than a carefully scoped adjacent method. Surveys identify concepts and primary sources; their headline savings are not independent experiments.

## Caveats missing or too weak in the original high-ranked cards

**ReflectiChain (#1 and #12).** These share author/method/benchmark lineage and should not be counted as independent replications. The short PDF also retains a LaTeX journal-template header; do not infer its publication status from that header.

The longer paper's §8.2–8.5, p20 and §10 p22 are essential: 68% extra tokens, possible conservatism, rationale unfaithfulness, keyword-based RCS and heuristic trajectory-based alpha estimates. Its printed SFI formula (§6 p10) sums weights to one but calls the range 0–100, omitting an explicit scaling operation. The violation term becomes negative for sufficiently many violations without a specified clipping rule. Its §3.2 contraction argument asserts pairwise contraction, while the preceding operational distance compares a state to constraints, and empirical alpha fitting is not a proof of a global contraction. These are reasons to require code-level definition, calibration and independent behavioral validation before operational use. They do not negate the basic observation that constraints can erode over time.

Most relevant to this project, **§7.10/Table9, pp17–18 reports negative transfer in CodeSim: CCR 40% for ReflectiChain versus 95% for ReAct; SFI 50.9 versus 70.7**. This is a small test (three episodes per benchmark/strategy), not a general comparative verdict. It is nevertheless stronger evidence against assuming code-domain benefit than the synthesis's supply-chain-only headline suggests. The draft vault note mentions simpler methods in unfamiliar domains, but the final report should present the concrete negative result. The paper's claim that reflection exceeds all other component contributions also omits the world-model contribution in its own comparison (§7.5 p14); additivity is not established by leave-one-out ablations in any case.

The proposed Librarian SFI calibration also says “8 landed drafts” while the final sprint baseline says nine delegate-drafted commits. More importantly, a few heterogeneous artifacts are not trajectory data from which a stable drift coefficient can be estimated. Start with exact behavioral constraint checks; keep semantic scores advisory.

**TMS (#2).** §4 p2 defines RU as active agent-time divided by **A×T**; a mean parallelism of 1.66 is not that fraction. §§4.1–4.3 include a tree/entity/validator mechanism worth extracting, but reported financial comparisons include assumptions about replacing human work, not an invoice-backed orchestrator saving. §5.8 pp14–15 gives coordination metrics without enough detail in that section for independent reproduction. On p16, the code link is still a placeholder and data are available on request. References repeatedly reuse `2305.16291` for Voyager, a different auction paper and HDDL2.1; that identifier's primary record is [Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291). Record the bibliography defect and reproducibility limit alongside its results.

**Mutual Influence (#6).** §5.1 pp6–7 reports increased agreement, but approval differences are not significant (normal p≈0.78; adversarial p≈0.46). Listing4 on p6 assigns feedback values explicitly; that is not evidence of a generally learned/calibrated trust mechanism. Runs were also filtered after timeouts. §5.2 p10 identifies groupthink risk. Extract rounds-to-approval as a proposed convergence measure, not a validated quality metric or evidence for a universal two-round cap.

**Confidence-aware verification (#8).** Retain the conditional error measures. Also extract the safety/utility frontier, abstention cost and the negative self-reflection control (§VI.G p11: no verdict flips in the stated 1,000 reflections). This does not show all self-reflection is ineffective; it tests that greedy self-check on those data. The model, training and visual-task specifics matter.

**HPR-GenRM (#9).** §9 p20 explicitly positions it as offline/batch reward labeling: 2,100 tokens and 8.4 seconds per query on four RTX4090s, reduced to 1,820/7.3 with adaptive depth. The residual step-label error floor is about 10% on a stratified audit, and cross-model tests concern open models in the 7–9B band. AppA.2 pp22–23 states conditional independence and noise assumptions; its effective-check-count approximation cannot be populated with our capability-label kappa. Use the architectural idea and uncertainty/benefit tradeoff, not the bound as a guarantee on heterogeneous Librarian verifiers.

**CoKV (#17).** Its units are attention heads and its implementation requires control of model inference/KV caches. That is unavailable through the current opaque API routes. Sliced Shapley credit for software-agent roles is a speculative analogy; plain paired lane ablations are cheaper and easier to interpret. It should not outrank direct verifier-cost evidence in a practical implementation queue.

**HDDLGym (#11).** Lack of a first-page identifier is a metadata-resolution issue, not grounds to exclude a relevant source. The [publisher record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/36143) resolves La, Mon-Williams and Shah, ICAPS 2025, DOI **10.1609/icaps.v35i1.36143**. Its deterministic/centralized-world assumptions and one-hot representation limitations (§7 pp8–9) should travel with its methods. No vault note was added in this review.

## What the next literature pass should require

For each included paper, require a section checklist covering mechanism, prerequisites, comparator, sample size/unit, total costs, ablations/negative results, limitations, source availability and application boundary. Record “not reported” rather than filling gaps. Preserve source hashes, actual PDF pages and source/version identifiers. Citation correctness, semantic support, source quality and extraction coverage need distinct fields.

Use three applicability levels: **direct candidate** (same class of control/verification problem), **bounded analogy** (transferable schema but different environment), and **out of scope** (training/physical phenomenon without a concrete transfer). Keep **unexamined** separate from irrelevant. Revisit excluded papers by section, especially formal verification and grounded evidence papers; do not screen them out merely because their application domain is not LLM orchestration.

These additions do not establish that every useful insight in the corpus has now been found. They do establish that the original six-card process missed material information, and provide a repeatable, more discriminating standard for completion.
