# Literature second pass and extraction supplement

The original extraction is **accurate at locating its selected quotations, but incomplete in useful methods, cost evidence and limitations**. Preserve its cards as indexed leads. Add the material below before publishing the synthesis or promoting the eleven draft vault notes to reviewed evidence.

Ranks below refer to the original fifty-paper reading list. Page numbers are **PDF pages**, not journal pagination. All applications to Librarian are proposals made in this review; the papers do not validate them for this application. The underlying PDF hashes match the sprint inventory. `data/literature_coverage.csv` gives a disposition for every ranked PDF; `data/supplement_methods.json` contains short anchors for the principal additions.

## Read in two tracks

For **metrics and orchestration**, prioritize #7 Embodied Tree of Thoughts, #14 the influence-policy compiler, #11 HDDLGym, #3 knowledge-driven mobile reasoning, and #8 confidence-aware verification. These offer concrete component costs, falsifiable baselines, explicit task effects and error trade-offs.

For **continuity and semantic drift**, retain #1/#12 ReflectiChain and #2 TMS, but read the limitations with the method. Treat their scores and claimed guarantees as candidates to validate, rather than defaults to implement. Read the primary Reflexion and Tree of Thoughts sources instead of relying solely on the vault's memory-written notes.

For **verification and architecture**, add #25 LLM-assisted combinatorial optimization, #42 rationale faithfulness, #28 static program queries, #26 service-composition traces, and #45 invariant-pattern automation. These are adjacent-domain methods with explicit transfer limits. #19 organisational knowledge work supplies human verification/coordination requirements, not an API cost model. #20 KDRAR, #33 LLM-S2KG and #43 STAGE also have bounded uses already recognized in the existing harness design; zero relevance in every category overstates their irrelevance to Librarian.

Avoid one total ranking that conflates domain match, method validity and immediate applicability. A directly named LLM-agent paper can be less reliable evidence than a carefully scoped adjacent method. Surveys identify concepts and primary sources; their headline savings are not independent experiments.

## Caveats missing or too weak in the original high-ranked cards

**ReflectiChain (#1 and #12).** These share author/method/benchmark lineage and should not be counted as independent replications. The short PDF also retains a LaTeX journal-template header; do not infer its publication status from that header.

The longer paper's §8.2–8.5, p20 and §10 p22 are essential: 68% extra tokens, possible conservatism, rationale unfaithfulness, keyword-based RCS and heuristic trajectory-based alpha estimates. Its printed SFI formula (§6 p10) sums weights to one but calls the range 0–100, omitting an explicit scaling operation. The violation term becomes negative for sufficiently many violations without a specified clipping rule. Its §3.2 contraction argument asserts pairwise contraction, while the preceding operational distance compares a state to constraints, and empirical alpha fitting is not a proof of a global contraction. These are reasons to require code-level definition, calibration and independent behavioral validation before operational use. They do not negate the basic observation that constraints can erode over time.

Most relevant to this project, **§7.10/Table9, pp17–18 reports negative transfer in CodeSim: CCR 40% for ReflectiChain versus 95% for ReAct; SFI 50.9 versus 70.7**. This is a small test (three episodes per benchmark/strategy), not a general comparative verdict. It is nevertheless stronger evidence against assuming code-domain benefit than the synthesis's supply-chain-only headline suggests. The draft vault note mentions simpler methods in unfamiliar domains, but the final report should present the concrete negative result. The paper's claim that reflection exceeds all other component contributions also omits the world-model contribution in its own comparison (§7.5 p14); additivity is not established by leave-one-out ablations in any case.

The proposed Librarian SFI calibration also says “8 landed drafts” while the final sprint baseline says nine delegate-drafted commits. More importantly, a few heterogeneous artifacts are not trajectory data from which a stable drift coefficient can be estimated. Start with exact behavioral constraint checks; keep semantic scores advisory.

**TMS (#2).** §4 p2 defines RU as active agent-time divided by **A×T**; a mean parallelism of 1.66 is not that fraction. §§4.1–4.3 include a tree/entity/validator mechanism worth extracting, but reported financial comparisons include assumptions about replacing human work, not an invoice-backed orchestrator saving. §5.8 pp14–15 gives coordination metrics without enough detail in that section for independent reproduction. On p16, the code link is still a placeholder and data are available on request. References repeatedly reuse `2305.16291` for Voyager, a different auction paper and HDDL2.1; that identifier's primary record is [Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291). Record the bibliography defect and reproducibility limit alongside its results.

**Mutual Influence (#6).** §5.1 pp6–7 reports increased agreement, but approval differences are not significant (normal p≈0.78; adversarial p≈0.46). Listing4 on p6 assigns feedback values explicitly; that is not evidence of a generally learned/calibrated trust mechanism. Runs were also filtered after timeouts. §5.2 p10 identifies groupthink risk. Extract rounds-to-approval as a proposed convergence measure, not a validated quality metric or evidence for a universal two-round cap.

**Confidence-aware verification (#8).** Retain the conditional error measures. Also extract the safety/utility frontier, abstention cost and the negative self-reflection control (§VI.G p11: no verdict flips in the stated 1,000 reflections). This does not show all self-reflection is ineffective; it tests that greedy self-check on those data. The model, training and visual-task specifics matter.

**HPR-GenRM (#9).** §9 p20 explicitly positions it as offline/batch reward labeling: 2,100 tokens and 8.4 seconds per query on four RTX4090s, reduced to 1,820/7.3 with adaptive depth. The residual step-label error floor is about 10% on a stratified audit, and cross-model tests concern open models in the 7–9B band. AppA.2 pp22–23 states conditional independence and noise assumptions; its effective-check-count approximation cannot be populated with our capability-label kappa. Use the architectural idea and uncertainty/benefit tradeoff, not the bound as a guarantee on heterogeneous Librarian verifiers.

**CoKV (#17).** Its units are attention heads and its implementation requires control of model inference/KV caches. That is unavailable through the current opaque API routes. Sliced Shapley credit for software-agent roles is a speculative analogy; plain paired lane ablations are cheaper and easier to interpret. It should not outrank direct verifier-cost evidence in a practical implementation queue.

**HDDLGym (#11).** Lack of a first-page identifier is a metadata-resolution issue, not grounds to exclude a relevant source. The [publisher record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/36143) resolves La, Mon-Williams and Shah, ICAPS 2025, DOI **10.1609/icaps.v35i1.36143**. Its deterministic/centralized-world assumptions and one-hot representation limitations (§7 pp8–9) should travel with its methods. No vault note was added in this review.

## What the next literature pass should require

For each included paper, require a section checklist covering mechanism, prerequisites, comparator, sample size/unit, total costs, ablations/negative results, limitations, source availability and application boundary. Record “not reported” rather than filling gaps. Preserve source hashes, actual PDF pages and source/version identifiers. Citation correctness, semantic support, source quality and extraction coverage need distinct fields.

Use three applicability levels: **direct candidate** (same class of control/verification problem), **bounded analogy** (transferable schema but different environment), and **out of scope** (training/physical phenomenon without a concrete transfer). Keep **unexamined** separate from irrelevant. Revisit excluded papers by section, especially formal verification and grounded evidence papers; do not screen them out merely because their application domain is not LLM orchestration.

These additions do not establish that every useful insight in the corpus has now been found. They do establish that the original six-card process missed material information, and provide a repeatable, more discriminating standard for completion.
